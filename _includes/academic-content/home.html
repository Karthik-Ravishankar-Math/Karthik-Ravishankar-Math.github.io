Computability theory & mathematical logic

I’m a fifth-year PhD student in mathematics at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, working with Joseph Miller in computability theory. Computability is a branch of mathematical logic that studies the limits of algorithms and Turing machines. By classifying problems according to their relative degrees of unsolvability, we obtain the rich structure of the Turing degrees, which calibrates computational strength.

I’m interested in local and global questions about the Turing and enumeration degrees. One current project characterizes levels of genericity through notions of computational strength. In computable structure theory, I’m studying degree spectra within the Δ02 degrees, as well as unions of spectra. Another project examines the information encodable in two jumps of a linear order.

I work in Lean and am interested in formalizing mathematics.

In past work on Ahmad pairs, I showed that the left half must be low3 and the right half high2. This separated the two halves and led to natural Π3 definitions of low3 and high2 in the local structure.
