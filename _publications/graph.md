The list distinguishing number <math aria-label="D subscript l of G"><msub><mi>D</mi><mi>l</mi></msub><mo>(</mo><mi>G</mi><mo>)</mo></math> measures how many colors each vertex’s allowed list must contain to guarantee a coloring that breaks every nontrivial symmetry. We show that list restrictions do not increase the distinguishing number of Cartesian powers <math aria-label="G to the r"><msup><mi>G</mi><mi>r</mi></msup></math> for connected Cartesian-prime graphs and <math aria-label="r is at least 3"><mi>r</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>3</mn></math>, and determine <math aria-label="D subscript l of Q subscript n to the bracket p"><msub><mi>D</mi><mi>l</mi></msub><mo>(</mo><msubsup><mi>Q</mi><mi>n</mi><mrow><mo>[</mo><mi>p</mi><mo>]</mo></mrow></msubsup><mo>)</mo></math> for powers of hypercubes with <math aria-label="n is at least 7"><mi>n</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>7</mn></math> and <math aria-label="p is at least 1"><mi>p</mi><mo>≥</mo><mn>1</mn></math>.
